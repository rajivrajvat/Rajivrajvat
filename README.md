## ◆ &nbsp;Who I Am

I work at the intersection of **technology, architecture, business and AI**.

Most of my career has been spent inside large organisations where the hard part is rarely
the code. It is understanding a process that nobody has written down, finding out who
actually owns it, and deciding which platform should carry it for the next five years.
That is the work I enjoy, and it is the work I have built a career around.

I don't approach technology as a collection of tools. A tool is the *last* decision, not
the first. I start with the business problem, map how the process really runs today,
design the architecture that fits it — identity, data, automation, integration — and only
then choose the technology that serves it best. Sometimes that is a Power App. Sometimes
it is a SharePoint information architecture and three flows. Sometimes the honest answer
is that the process needs fixing before any system is built.

What I bring is the ability to hold both ends of that line. I can sit with a business
stakeholder and translate what they need into a technical design, and then go and build
it myself. I have led delivery, mentored engineers and run technical reviews — but I have
never stopped being hands-on, because architecture that has never survived
implementation is just a diagram.

## ◆ &nbsp;Architecture & Engineering

<table>
<tr><td width="50%" valign="top">

**▪ Requirements before technology**
The platform is chosen to fit the problem. When the decision runs the other way, every
later compromise traces back to it.

**▪ Architecture before implementation**
Components, boundaries and integration paths get agreed while they are still cheap to
change.

**▪ Identity and security as design inputs**
Access model, roles and data classification belong in the first design conversation.
Security added at the end is a patch, not a posture.

**▪ Deliberate platform selection**
SharePoint, Dataverse, SQL and Azure each have a job they do best. Knowing when *not* to
reach for a platform is as valuable as knowing how to use it.

**▪ Integration as a first-class concern**
Most enterprise value sits between systems. Interfaces, contracts and failure behaviour
are designed, not discovered in production.

</td><td width="50%" valign="top">

**▪ Data architecture that survives growth**
A model built around how the business actually thinks outlasts one built around the first
screen someone asked for.

**▪ Automation where it removes real effort**
Automating a broken process makes it fail faster. Fix the process, then automate it.

**▪ Maintainability over cleverness**
The next engineer — often a colleague, sometimes me in a year — must be able to change it
safely. Clever is a cost.

**▪ Governance and lifecycle**
Environments, policy, ownership and change control decide whether a solution is still
trusted in year three.

**▪ Production readiness is the finish line**
Deployed, adopted, supportable and documented. Anything short of that is a prototype,
however good the demo was.

</td></tr>
</table>

## ◆ &nbsp;AI & Digital Transformation

> **AI is not the destination. Business transformation is.**
> AI is one of the technologies that gets you there — and one of the easiest to spend
> money on without changing anything.

My interest in AI is in **where it genuinely creates value**, and in being honest about
where it does not. The questions I ask first are the unglamorous ones: what decision does
this actually accelerate, who is accountable for the output, what happens when the model
is wrong, and is the underlying information good enough to ground an answer at all.

<table>
<tr><td width="33%" valign="top">

**⬡ Enterprise AI & agents**
Conversational and agentic solutions built with Copilot Studio and Azure AI over content
the organisation already governs — so the answer comes from the system of record, not
from somewhere adjacent to it.

</td><td width="33%" valign="top">

**⬡ AI + automation together**
The durable pattern is not a chatbot. It is AI handling the judgement-shaped step inside
an automated workflow that still has routing, approval and an audit trail around it.

</td><td width="33%" valign="top">

**⬡ Grounding over generation**
RAG and retrieval patterns exist so a model answers from your material rather than its
imagination. Information architecture is what makes that work — which is why this is a
SharePoint problem as much as an AI one.

</td></tr>
<tr><td width="33%" valign="top">

**⬡ Human in the loop, by design**
In the AI-assisted authoring workflows I have built, the model proposes and a person
reviews and applies. Nothing generated reaches an audience unreviewed, and nothing
generated can quietly overwrite work a human wrote.

</td><td width="33%" valign="top">

**⬡ Provider-agnostic thinking**
Models change faster than enterprises can re-platform. I design the model as a
replaceable component behind a stable interface, so a better one is an upgrade rather
than a rewrite.

</td><td width="33%" valign="top">

**⬡ AI governance**
Where output is stored, what it may and may not change, how sources are verified, and how
you demonstrate afterwards that a person was accountable. Certified across applied and
strategic AI — AB-100, AI-103, AB-730, AB-731.

</td></tr>
</table>

## ◆ &nbsp;Business & Technology

The most useful thing I do is translation. Requirements arrive as frustration and
workaround; they have to leave as an architecture someone can build and a business case
someone can approve.

<table>
<tr><th width="42%">The business says</th><th width="58%">I turn it into</th></tr>
<tr><td>"Approvals take too long and nobody knows where a request is."</td><td>A workflow with defined states, role-based ownership, automated routing and escalation, and visible status at every stage.</td></tr>
<tr><td>"Every team keeps its own version of this data."</td><td>A single source of truth with an access model, integration into the systems that need it, and reporting built on top of it.</td></tr>
<tr><td>"This system works, but we can't change anything."</td><td>A modernisation path — assessed, staged and reversible — that reaches a supported platform without a big-bang cutover.</td></tr>
<tr><td>"We need reports for the leadership review."</td><td>A data model designed for the questions being asked, with dashboards refreshed automatically rather than assembled by hand.</td></tr>
<tr><td>"Can we use AI for this?"</td><td>An honest answer: where AI adds measurable value here, where automation alone is sufficient and cheaper, and what has to be true about the data first.</td></tr>
<tr><td>"Audit needs evidence of who did what."</td><td>Identity, permissions and traceability designed into the solution — so evidence is a query, not a reconstruction.</td></tr>
</table>

## ◆ &nbsp;Where I'm Heading

The next decade of enterprise technology will be decided by how well organisations
combine **automation, AI and good architecture** — and by how many of them can tell the
difference between a capability and a demo.

That is where I am focused: **agentic AI inside the Microsoft ecosystem**, Copilot-era
